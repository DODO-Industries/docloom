# Atri's Roadmap: Research, GATE, PhD, and Business
Written 2026-08-02, from a full planning conversation. This is the working
plan to refer back to — update it as real outcomes (GATE result, company
policy, first business clients) come in, rather than replanning from zero.

## The one rule that keeps this from collapsing

At any given time, **exactly two things are primary** (get your best hours);
everything else runs in maintenance (scheduled, non-zero, not where your
energy goes). The phases below say which two, when.

---

## Phase 1 — Now through GATE

**Primary**: Job (income + stability) + GATE prep (maths, probability/stats,
DBMS — the confirmed remaining gaps; AI/ML already solid). This matches the
**GATE DA (Data Science & AI)** paper specifically: ML + Probability/Stats
are the two heaviest-weighted sections (~40% combined), Linear Algebra ~13%.

**Maintenance, running in parallel, not paused for GATE**:
- Finish DocLoom's benchmarking + write it up as a real paper. Publish it
  open (arXiv first — immediate, no peer review needed, still citable),
  then aim it at a real, low-barrier venue (MemAgents @ ICLR, 2-page
  track). This is not a detour from GATE — it's the asset that helps
  *every* later door: GATE-independent PhD admission, professor outreach,
  and the business's technical credibility, all at once.
- Start the emergency fund (target: 6 months of basic expenses) and the
  marriage fund as two separate, fixed monthly transfers the moment salary
  lands — not what's left over at month-end.
- Keep the professor relationship(s) warm with light, occasional contact —
  not a full pitch yet, just staying a known quantity.

**Business does not start yet in this phase** — see Phase 2 for why the
timing matters.

---

## Phase 2 — The business: start it, but start it lean

You're right that this needs to start now, not wait for GATE or the PhD —
the job has no growth ceiling and the business is the actual income-growth
engine. Here's how to start it without it competing with GATE/job for
hours it doesn't have:

**Start as R&D-driven consulting, not a maintained product.** Companies
pay you directly for project work (RAG pipelines, embedding systems,
memory layers for their agents) using what DocLoom taught you — no
ongoing support/uptime burden, fits evening/weekend hours.

**What to build/pitch, specifically** — not the "second brain" story
(too crowded, funded competitors already own that narrative: Mem0, Zep,
Letta). Lead with DocLoom's two **measured, real** wins instead:

1. **"RAG that doesn't go stale."** Sell the knowledge-updating/
   supersession capability. Every company running RAG over internal docs
   has this exact pain — policies/prices/specs change, the bot keeps
   confidently serving the old answer. You have a real number:
   current-fact retrieval, FAISS 0.15 vs DocLoom 1.00. Concrete, painful,
   fundable.
2. **"Finds what plain search misses."** The multi-hop/associative
   recall win (answer causally linked to the query but not textually
   similar) — useful for support/knowledge-base tools specifically.

**Pick ONE narrow vertical, not "memory for everyone."** Compliance/
policy-heavy internal tools, or customer support for products with
frequent updates, or internal engineering wikis — anywhere "stale answers
cost real money." A funded competitor selling generically is hard to
out-market solo; a specific, proven niche isn't their focus.

**Pricing**: usage/credit-based (per query or per ingested document),
piloted with 1-2 small companies/startups first — not enterprise, whose
sales cycles are too long for the hours you have available right now.

**The R&D/IP side, honestly:**
- Papers first, always — arXiv + workshop submissions build the
  technical credibility that sells the consulting work, and cost nothing
  but writing time you're already spending on the DocLoom paper.
- Patents: **worth checking, not assuming.** The broad ideas here
  (activation decay, causal-graph spreading, self-organizing consolidation)
  sit on dense prior art (ACT-R, spreading-activation theory, standard
  LSM/WAL storage engineering) — a *broad* patent is unlikely to hold up.
  A patent on a **specific, narrow technical implementation** (e.g., the
  exact coherence-gated causal-edge mechanism, or the faithfulness-gated
  consolidation loop, if you build them) is a different, more realistic
  question — worth 30 minutes with a real patent attorney once you have a
  concrete implementation to describe, not a guess made here.

**Growth check-ins, not a fixed deadline**: aim to have 2-3 real paying
clients within the first year of consulting. If revenue is meaningfully
closing the gap with the job's ₹80k/month by the end of year 2, that's
the signal the timing/shape is working. If not, that's the point to
rethink the plan, not push through on hope.

## Phase 3 — GATE result branches the path

**If GATE clears:**
- Shift the business timeline by ~1 year to make room: pursue PhD (via
  JRF — real stipend: ₹37,000/month years 1-2 + ₹20,000/year grant, rising
  to ₹42,000/month + ₹25,000/year grant from year 3) alongside the job if
  the company permits a distance/part-time arrangement — check this
  specifically with your employer, since JRF stipend applies to full-time
  enrollment, and a distance mode may mean self-funding the PhD fees
  instead while keeping the salary.
- If the company does **not** allow distance PhD arrangements: go
  full-time PhD instead, and let the business start in 2027 regardless
  (it doesn't depend on the PhD route chosen) — consulting hours can flex
  around a full-time PhD's schedule more easily than around a full-time
  job's, in most cases.
- Note honestly: a distance/part-time PhD is real and solves the money
  problem, but is generally viewed as lighter for landing a strong
  Assistant Professor post later than a full-time program — a real
  trade-off to accept knowingly, not by default.

**If GATE does not clear (first attempt or at all) — the plan does not
collapse, because only one branch depended on it:**
- Retry it — most people clear GATE on a 2nd/3rd attempt, not the first.
- UGC-NET is a separate exam toward the same JRF/PhD-funding goal —
  worth preparing both, since the subject overlap is large.
- Direct-entry PhD via research proposal + interview is real, especially
  backed by a published paper — several institutes admit this way without
  a GATE/NET score, when research capability is demonstrated.
- Abroad remains open regardless of GATE (see Phase 4).
- Worst case: no PhD ever happens. The business + the published work
  still stand as a real career on their own — the PhD was one path to
  "professor," not the only path to a good career.

## Phase 4 — Abroad, and family

Family being in India is the deciding factor, and that's a legitimate
priority to hold, not something to trade against money. Plan as: don't
actively pursue abroad applications, but don't burn the door either — if
an admission genuinely comes through (e.g., you apply on a whim, or a
professor relationship leads there), reconsider at that point rather than
foreclosing it now.

---

## Phase 5 — Post-PhD (whichever route), and the long professor arc

- India: apply for Assistant Professor positions. Abroad: usually a
  postdoc first, then tenure-track Assistant Professor.
- "Professor" (the senior rank) is a later promotion (Assistant →
  Associate → Professor), typically 10-15+ years into a faculty career —
  not the title you get right after the PhD. Plan patience into this
  stage specifically.
- By this point, if the business has gone well, it may be its own
  income stream independent of an academic salary — which is the honest
  way "professor AND financially secure" actually happens: sequenced,
  with the business given real years to mature, not chased simultaneously
  with everything else from day one.

---

## The through-line

DocLoom's paper is the one asset that touches every branch: it's evidence
for GATE-independent PhD admission, it's the professor-outreach centerpiece,
and it's the technical credibility behind the consulting business. Finishing
it well, honestly (including the negative growth-sweep result), is the
single highest-leverage thing on this entire roadmap — everything else
branches off having that done.
