DOCLOOM — ONE-PAGE RESEARCH PITCH (for professor outreach)
2026-08-02. Every number below was re-run live today against a fresh
benchmark cache — nothing here is recycled from old notes without
re-verification.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Vector databases (FAISS, HNSW, and the RAG systems built on them) are
frozen snapshots: you embed a corpus once, build an index, and the
index's notion of "what's true" never changes unless you rebuild it.
Two things break under that model, structurally, not as a tuning issue:

  1. UPDATING KNOWLEDGE. When a fact changes (a document is revised, a
     policy is superseded), a static index has no idea the old vector
     is stale — it will keep serving it, ranked exactly as confidently
     as the correction.
  2. MULTI-HOP / ASSOCIATIVE RECALL. When the correct answer is not
     textually similar to the query — it's reachable only through an
     intermediate fact — flat cosine similarity cannot find it at all,
     by construction, regardless of how the index is tuned.

DocLoom is a living memory substrate (append-only, self-organizing
storage + a lightweight cognitive layer: activation decay, causal-graph
edges between co-accessed facts, LLM-driven consolidation) built to
test whether a memory system can do BOTH of those things a frozen
index structurally cannot.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
MEASURED RESULT (re-verified today, same corpus/queries as FAISS)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Two axes where DocLoom beats FAISS not by tuning but by construction:

  UPDATING: 20 facts changed v1->v2, old version marked superseded.
    current-fact@1:      FAISS 0.15   |   DocLoom 1.00
    stale-ranked-above-current (wrong answer surfaces first):
                          FAISS 0.85   |   DocLoom 0.00
  FAISS has no mechanism for "this vector is outdated" — it will
  confidently return the wrong, superseded answer 85% of the time.

  MULTI-HOP: answer causally linked to the query but not textually
  similar to it (genuine 2-hop bridge items, programmatically
  generated, with a measured isolation check confirming cosine
  literally cannot reach the answer for the hard subset).
    answer@5, full set:        cosine 0.55   |   DocLoom 0.80
    answer@5, hard subset (cosine-unreachable by construction):
                                cosine 0.00   |   DocLoom 0.56

Where DocLoom currently loses, honestly: raw ingest/query throughput
(FAISS ~20-40x faster per operation, because it's doing strictly less
work per item), and a "does recall quality improve as the store grows"
growth-sweep run today came back NEGATIVE under benign growth (both
systems' recall dipped similarly as unrelated material was added; the
store's own self-organization/division mechanism did not engage at the
tested scale). That result is not hidden here — it's the open question
below.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
THE OPEN RESEARCH QUESTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
No published continual-memory system has demonstrated retrieval
quality that holds or improves as the store grows under ADVERSARIAL,
interference-dominated growth (streams of contradicting/superseding
facts at scale), with the improvement causally attributed — via
ablation — to a specific consolidation mechanism, and independently
audited for consolidation faithfulness (does the system's own summary
of what it learned actually match what happened, or does it quietly
hallucinate). DocLoom already has the two structural ingredients this
needs (supersession tracking + causal-graph consolidation) and the
already-measured wins above suggest the mechanism is real — the
missing piece is the rigorous, adversarial-scale experiment to prove
(or honestly kill) the growth claim itself.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THE GROWTH RESULT WAS NEGATIVE, AND THE ACTUAL CONTRIBUTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Diagnosing today's negative result surfaced the real gap: every
reorganization decision in the current system is gated by an arbitrary
fixed threshold, not a signal derived from the substrate's own state —
crystals split on a raw item COUNT (500,000), sleep/consolidation fires
on a CLOCK, a consolidated summary ships regardless of whether a
faithfulness check would reject it, and forgetting is a silent delete
with no record of what was known or why it faded.

The proposed contribution is to replace each fixed threshold with a
signal the substrate already computes about itself:
  - split a memory region when its internal COHERENCE drops (it got
    confused), not when its count crosses a number (it got big);
  - consolidate when a region's accumulated SURPRISE/prediction-error
    crosses a threshold, not on a clock;
  - gate consolidation on its own measured FAITHFULNESS score inline
    (reject/retry a hallucinating synthesis) instead of only reporting
    it after the fact;
  - keep a compressed, auditable trace of what was forgotten and why,
    instead of a bare delete — "graceful forgetting with provenance,"
    a metric no published memory-store system currently reports.

This is the honest thesis-level claim: not "our physics beats cosine,"
but "self-organization driven by the substrate's own state beats
self-organization driven by arbitrary fixed thresholds" — directly
testable by re-running the growth-sweep once the count-based division
trigger is replaced with the coherence-based one above.
